# Unit 4 Finding: The Real Spam Rate and Where It Concentrates

**To:** The leadership team
**From:** Nicholas Ku, Trust and Data

The true spam withdrawal rate is 6 percent. Of the 200 ratings on the platform, 12 were withdrawn as spam, which matches what I found in the last audit. The rate for every movie is calculated here against all of that movie's ratings, so the numbers below can be compared with each other and with the 6 percent platform figure.

The rate is not the same for every movie. Four movies (5, 8, 10 and 13) have no spam withdrawals at all, and seven more have exactly one. Movie 2 stands out with 3 spam withdrawals out of 16 ratings, or 19 percent, and movie 3 has 2 out of 13, or 15 percent. For a fair comparison I took the 12 movies that have at least 10 ratings and combined their ratings, which gives a typical rate of 5.7 percent. Movie 2 is about 13 points above that and movie 3 is about 10 points above. Movie 4 has 1 spam withdrawal out of 6 ratings, which looks high, but 6 ratings is too few to say anything. By region, India has the highest rate at 10 percent (4 out of 40) and Vietnam and the UK have the lowest at about 2 percent.

I would investigate movies 2 and 3. I would also keep an eye on movie 4, but only to see whether it collects more ratings before anyone draws a conclusion from it. I want to be careful about how much weight these numbers carry, because the counts are small. If spam were spread evenly, movie 2 would be expected to have about one spam withdrawal, and it has three. With 12 movies in the comparison, it is roughly as likely as not that at least one of them reaches three by chance. I would treat movies 2 and 3 as places to start looking and not as proof that anything is wrong with them. A reviewer can check who submitted those five spam ratings and when, which would show quickly whether it is a coordinated effort or a few unrelated cases.

One caution about the average scores on the dashboard. Every withdrawn rating has a score of exactly 0.5, which looks like a placeholder, so any average that includes withdrawn ratings is pulled down. I can't tell from the data whether users really gave that score. The platform average of 2.85 becomes 3.32 when only published ratings are counted. I would not use average score to judge a movie or its reviewers until the 0.5 values are explained.
